# Welcome to the Makerspace

The Makerspace is a 4,500 sq ft shared fabrication facility in the Innovation Centre at Ashoka University. It gives students, researchers, staff, and approved participants a place to move from an idea to a prototype, a repair, an experiment, or a better question.

You do not need to arrive as an expert. The space exists to make the joy of building, repairing, and learning with your hands more accessible. Tools matter, but the real resource is a community that shares skills face to face.

## What you can do here

- Learn a fabrication process from the beginning.
- Build and test a prototype.
- Repair an object instead of replacing it.
- Make a custom part for a project.
- Work across electronics, textiles, wood, digital fabrication, computing, and visualisation.
- Find collaborators whose skills are different from yours.
- Document a useful result so somebody else can build on it.

## Your first visit

1. **Contact the team.** Confirm current access arrangements, open hours, and whether your project needs a booking.
2. **Complete fire and safety training.** This is required before machine access.
3. **Take the machine primer.** Each machine has its own operating procedure and approval process.
4. **Start with a small test.** A scrap piece or rough prototype usually teaches more than a long argument with yourself.
5. **Ask and share.** Talk to mentors and other makers, then leave useful notes for the next person.

:::warning Training comes before machine access
Reading a manual is good preparation, but it does not replace supervised training, a machine-specific primer, or staff approval.
:::

## How to ask for help

Bring three things to the conversation:

- what you want to happen;
- what is happening instead; and
- what you have already tried.

A sketch, photo, failed part, error message, or odd noise can be more helpful than a polished explanation. Nobody in the room knows everything. The useful skill is knowing how to find the next person, test, or resource.

## A shared workshop, not a vending machine

The Makerspace works when people learn safely, return tools, label work, clean the bench, share credit, and help knowledge move through the community. A strong project is welcome. A strong project that teaches somebody else something is even better.
